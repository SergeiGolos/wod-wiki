# HYROX 12-Week Training Program — Overview
**Source:** PureGym 12-week HYROX plan by PT and Cluster Manager Andrea Marshall (puregym.com/blog/hyrox-training-plan, Sept 2024; updated Sept 2025). Freely published; one of the few public programs with full day-by-day sessions.

The plan is three cycles of a four-week block, built to peak on race day. Cycle 1 (Weeks 1-4) lays the foundation, Cycle 2 (Weeks 5-8) repeats it heavier and faster, Cycle 3 (Weeks 9-12) peaks — with Week 12 mirroring Week 4 as a deload at lighter loads and its Friday simulation becoming an extra rest day.
## What the Program Trains
- **Running (~50% of race time):** one interval session (8 × 1 km with equal rest), one Zone 2 run, and running threaded through every simulation. Hyrox is a runner's game.
- **Compromised work:** stations arrive after a 1 km run, so key sessions are AMRAP/EMOM complexes that train output under fatigue.
- **Grip:** farmers carries, sled pulls, and heavy dumbbell work build the grip endurance the last four stations demand.
- **Simulations:** twice per cycle, half-simulations (first four or last four stations, each preceded by a 1 km run) rehearse race pace and ROXzone transitions.
- **Strength:** sled, sandbag, and barbell/dumbbell complexes targeting the muscle groups behind each station.
## Weekly Rhythm (template week)
- **Monday:** strength or endurance complex
- **Tuesday:** endurance (AMRAP stations)
- **Wednesday:** strength / performance or rest
- **Thursday:** performance, intervals, or rest
- **Friday:** rest or half-simulation
- **Saturday:** intervals or easy 5 km
- **Sunday:** Zone 2 run, performance repeat, or rest
## Short on Time: the 8-Week Version
For athletes who already train, the source prescribes compressing each fortnight of the 12-week plan into one week: one interval session, one strength/skills circuit, one compromised run, and one easy run + mobility day per week. Milestones: half-simulation at the halfway point, a full simulation at lighter loads in week 6, taper in the final week. Keep at least two rest days per week and stick to Open weights.
## Taper
Final week: reduce training volume 40-50% but keep a little speed work to stay sharp. Rest and recovery are as important as the hard sessions.
